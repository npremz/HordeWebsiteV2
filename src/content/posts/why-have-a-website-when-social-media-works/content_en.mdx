## The short answer: both, but for different jobs

You probably need both. Social media distributes content, creates connection and lets you test ideas quickly. Your website turns that attention into lasting visibility, enquiries and data you control. For an initial offer, that foundation may be a focused [landing page built to convert](/en/services/creation-landing-page/) rather than a fifty-page website.

The decision is not really “website or social media”. The useful question is: **which channel is the foundation of the business, and which one amplifies it?**

| Criterion | Website | Social media |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ownership | You control the domain and experience | The platform sets the rules |
| Visibility | Search traffic and durable content | Reach tied to feeds and algorithms |
| Lifespan | Months or years | Often hours or days |
| Conversion | Custom journeys and forms | Options defined by the platform |
| Data | Direct measurement and owned contacts | Partial access to audience data |
| Relationship | Email, CRM and a portable audience | Audience attached to the account |

## Why relying on social media alone creates dependency

You post regularly. Your followers are growing. Clients find you through Instagram or LinkedIn. That is valuable, but the better one channel performs, the more expensive an interruption becomes.

When social media is your only presence, every platform change affects you directly: a new algorithm, different rules, falling reach, a moderation error or an account suspension.

Meta said it removed more than 100 million fake Pages involved in scripted follows abuse in **2024**. The figure appeared in [an April 2025 update from Meta](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/04/cracking-down-spammy-content-facebook/) about spam and distribution changes. Rival IQ’s 2025 benchmarks also showed declining organic engagement across several major platforms.

The point is not that Instagram or LinkedIn will disappear. It is that a business cannot control the decisions those platforms make.

## Digital sharecropping

This dependency is often called **digital sharecropping**. You invest time, energy and money in an audience built on someone else's land.

On a social platform, you do not fully control:

* **Who sees the content.** Reach changes according to rules you cannot influence.
* **The context around the brand.** Posts appear beside ads, competitors and distractions.
* **Access to the audience.** You receive analytics, but not a direct, portable relationship with every follower.
* **The content's lifespan.** A post quickly disappears from the feed, while a useful page can be found for years.

Platforms can redistribute organic reach whenever their algorithms change. A company that depends entirely on that visibility cannot fully control its acquisition costs or access to its audience.

## What a website actually adds

A website is not just a more expensive business card. It creates a space where the message, journey and conversion points belong to you.

**Your brand.** You decide the hierarchy, design and context. There are no competing ads or imposed formats around the offer.

**Your audience.** A form, newsletter or booking flow turns an anonymous visitor into a direct relationship. That list remains available if a platform changes its rules.

**Your visibility.** A useful page can appear when someone actively searches for your service on Google. It does not depend entirely on a publishing schedule.

**Your data.** You can measure page visits, traffic sources and conversions with tools you choose.

**Your credibility.** The [DreamHost 2026 Local Business Trust Index](https://www.dreamhost.com/blog/dreamhost-2026-local-business-trust-index/) reported that 69% of surveyed consumers considered a website essential to local-business credibility. In B2B, a [Gartner survey published in March 2026](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-03-09-gartner-sales-survey-finds-67-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-experience) found that 67% of surveyed buyers preferred a sales-representative-free experience. Those findings do not make a website mandatory in every situation; they help explain why prospects often want to verify a business at their own pace.

## When social media can be enough temporarily

Not every business needs a large website on day one. Social media can be a reasonable starting point when:

* you are testing an activity and the offer is still changing;
* sales depend mainly on an existing community;
* the activity is temporary or event-based, although a [bespoke event web app](/en/blog/ai-assisted-event-web-apps/) can become useful when the experience needs live control or synchronisation;
* you do not yet have content, references or a clear sales journey.

Even then, securing a domain and publishing one simple page reduces the risk of depending entirely on a social profile.

## When a website becomes essential

### You sell B2B services

Prospects need to understand the expertise, compare the process, see previous work and share the website with other decision-makers. A social profile structures that research poorly.

### Customers search for you locally

A company discovered through Google Maps or local search needs a credible destination with services, evidence and a clear way to make contact.

### You sell online

The catalogue, payments, logistics and customer journey need infrastructure designed for transactions. Social platforms can present products, while the [online store](/en/services/creation-ecommerce/) remains the commercial engine.

### You invest in advertising

Sending a campaign to a page where you control the message, speed and measurement makes conversion easier to improve. A [custom landing page](/en/services/creation-landing-page/) is often more effective than a general social profile.

### You want content to compound over time

Guides, case studies and answers to customer questions can attract visitors long after publication. On social media, the same content is difficult to find and organise.

## The minimum website when social media already works

You do not necessarily need fifty pages. A useful first version can contain:

* a clear promise and the audience it serves;
* the main services or products;
* evidence such as work, results, reviews or references;
* the people behind the business;
* a form, email address or booking option;
* privacy-conscious measurement;
* a structure that can accommodate new pages later.

The right minimum website is not a cheap, disposable website. It is a deliberately focused foundation built to evolve.

For it to be genuinely yours, check the less visible parts too: the domain is registered to the business, your team has administrator access, content and contacts can be exported, measurement tools remain accessible and the contract explains what happens if you change supplier.

## The right balance: home base and amplifiers

**Your website is the home base.** You control the message, experience, data and relationship with visitors. It is where Google can index the content and where prospects verify the business.

**Social media is the amplifier.** It shows personality, creates conversation and distributes ideas to a community.

A resilient journey looks like this:

1. Someone discovers your content on a social platform.
2. They visit a page that develops the idea or presents the offer.
3. They subscribe, book a call or send an enquiry.
4. The relationship continues through email or your CRM instead of depending on the feed.

## A practical 90-day path

Everything does not have to be built at once. A small business can move in stages and verify the usefulness of each investment.

**Month one: secure the foundation.** Register the domain, publish a clear page with the offer, evidence and a contact route, then confirm that conversions are being measured.

**Month two: organise distribution.** Select one or two platforms that the intended audience genuinely uses. Each important post should lead to a relevant page or resource instead of sending every visitor to the homepage.

**Month three: build a durable asset.** Turn recurring sales questions into pages, case studies or guides. Begin an email list with clear consent and connect useful contacts to the CRM.

At the end of 90 days, compare channels by the qualified enquiries they generated, not only impressions and follower counts. That evidence shows where the next investment belongs.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a website if Instagram already brings clients?

Not necessarily a large one, but you should have a foundation you own. It gives prospects somewhere to verify the offer, find you outside Instagram and make contact if the account becomes less visible.

### Can a Google Business Profile replace a website?

It is extremely useful for a local business, but limited when you need to explain several services, publish content, present case studies and build a conversion journey.

### Should I start with a website or social media?

Start by clarifying the offer and sales journey. A focused page and one primary social channel can then launch together. The website holds stable information; the social channel creates rhythm and distribution.

### Does social media improve Google rankings?

It can increase content visibility and generate visits or links, but follower and like counts do not replace relevant pages, sound SEO architecture and a strong reputation across the web.
