## The short answer

At Horde, a professional service-website redesign in Belgium generally costs **between €2,000 and €8,000**. Focused optimisation on a sound foundation can remain below €2,000. A large multilingual website, an online store or a project with business integrations may exceed **€8,000 to €20,000**, sometimes by a wide margin.

The difference is not just the number of pages. It comes from what needs to be reconsidered: content, user journeys, design, SEO migration, the CMS and connections to the rest of your business.

| Project type | Indicative budget | Usually included |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Optimisation or light intervention | €500 to €2,000 | Focused fixes, limited adaptation, client-supplied content |
| Professional service-website redesign | €2,000 to €8,000 | Audit, UX, design, development, technical SEO and launch |
| Multilingual, e-commerce or integrations | €8,000 to €20,000+ | Migration, catalogue, languages, connections and business logic |

These are practical ranges drawn from our Belgian proposals and projects in 2026, not an official market average or an automatic price list. Two €6,000 proposals can cover very different amounts of work.

## Website redesign price ranges in Belgium

### Optimisation, template or light intervention: €500 to €2,000

A website assembled with Wix, Webflow or WordPress and a purchased theme can be enough to test an idea or publish a few pages quickly. The budget stays low because the structure, components and much of the design already exist.

The limits appear as the company grows: average performance, little differentiation, rigid journeys and dependence on plugins. That does not make it the wrong choice, but the limitations need to be understood before comparing it with custom work.

### Professional service-website redesign: €2,000 to €8,000

This is where deeper work becomes possible: auditing the existing site, planning content and architecture, creating a custom design, developing it carefully and protecting existing SEO value.

It is also where you find people who examine the business objective before choosing a CMS or drawing a page. This is our usual territory at Horde. Our [website redesign service](/en/services/website-redesign-brussels/) explains how we approach it.

### Multilingual, e-commerce or integrated project: €8,000 to €20,000+

E-commerce websites with large catalogues, client portals, large multilingual sites, CRM or ERP integrations and booking platforms cost more because the technical and organisational complexity is real.

In this category, data, permissions, redirects and integrations can require more work than the screens visitors eventually see.

## Three realistic budget examples

### A 5-to-10-page service website

For a small company with usable existing content, a redesign covering architecture, custom design, development, a lightweight CMS and SEO foundations often falls between **€2,000 and €6,000** at Horde.

The budget rises when the agency also has to reposition the offer, write the pages or produce photography and video.

### A multilingual website with an SEO migration

A 30-to-50-page French-English website that already receives organic traffic requires a URL inventory, redirect mapping, metadata migration and editorial review in both languages. Depending on the content and level of customisation, it can fall between **€6,000 and €12,000**.

### An online store with payments and logistics

A customised Shopify store with a catalogue, payments, delivery and e-commerce SEO starts above a standard service website. ## What actually changes the quote

### The volume and state of the content

Taking over 80 poorly structured pages, reformatting images and rewriting metadata takes time. Many companies arrive with websites where most pages overlap. Before development starts, someone has to decide what stays, what merges and what disappears.

### The SEO migration

If the current website already generates organic traffic, a poorly prepared redesign can cause fluctuations and make useful pages disappear. [Google recommends](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/site-move-with-url-changes) preparing a URL map, testing redirects, updating links and sitemaps, then monitoring both the old and new site in Search Console.

This work is not optional when existing visibility matters.

### User journeys and UX work

A beautiful website can still send visitors in the wrong direction. Rethinking navigation, conversion points, forms and information hierarchy requires understanding users before producing screens.

### Third-party integrations

CRM connections, automations, booking, payments, member areas and catalogue synchronisation each bring their own constraints. Some take a few hours. Others require several days of development and testing across multiple teams.

### The CMS and long-term maintenance

The launch price is not the total cost. A well-chosen CMS lets the team update content without calling a developer for every small change. A poor foundation can look cheaper today and become much more expensive two years later.

## What a serious redesign should include

Before comparing two quotes, make sure they cover the same work.

* Audit of the website, objectives and available data
* Page architecture and priority user journeys
* Responsive design adapted to the brand
* Development and performance optimisation, with targets connected to [Core Web Vitals](/en/blog/what-is-a-really-fast-website/)
* Content migration and SEO redirects
* CMS configuration and team training
* Testing across common devices and browsers
* Launch, quality control and a follow-up period

Anything missing from the proposal either needs to be handled elsewhere or removed from the scope deliberately.

### A simple grid for comparing two proposals

| Area | Question to ask |
| --- | --- |
| Scope | Which pages, languages and features are explicitly included? |
| Content | Who inventories, writes, translates, enters and approves the content? |
| Design | How many templates and feedback rounds are planned? |
| Technical work | Are the CMS, hosting, licences and integrations specified? |
| SEO | Who prepares redirects, metadata, canonicals and indexation checks? |
| Quality | Which tests will be performed and on which devices or browsers? |
| After launch | Are training, corrections and monitoring included, and for how long? |

This grid makes exclusions visible. It prevents a visual-design-only proposal from being compared with work that also includes content, development, migration and post-launch monitoring.

## The costs quotes often leave out

**Content writing.** Most agencies do not automatically write every page. If your team cannot supply the text, someone has to research and produce it. A practical range is €80 to €200 per page, depending on the depth of research and SEO work.

**Brand assets.** Photography, video, illustration and iconography can become a separate budget line.

**Hosting and external services.** Domain registration, hosting, transactional email, licences and measurement tools often represent €100 to €500 per year for a standard website, and more for a complex platform.

**Post-launch maintenance.** Updates, backups, performance monitoring and smaller improvements require internal time or a maintenance agreement.

**Your team's time.** Briefing, supplying content, approvals and testing can easily require 15 to 30 hours from the client during a serious redesign.

## The mistakes that make budgets explode

**Signing before defining the scope.** “Redesign the website” can mean five pages to the agency and fifty pages, a blog and a client portal to you.

**Changing direction halfway through.** A decision made before design costs little. The same decision after development may require several stages to be repeated.

**Choosing on price alone.** A proposal sent without an audit, questions about the business or a review of the current website is difficult to compare with a complete process.

**Ignoring SEO migration.** A better design does not compensate for lost pages, links and Google rankings.

## Full redesign or focused optimisation?

Not every imperfect website needs rebuilding. Focused optimisation can be enough when the structure is sound, the CMS still works and the problems concern speed, a few key pages or the conversion journey.

A redesign becomes relevant when several problems overlap: confusing architecture, weak credibility, poor performance, an obstructive CMS, unmanageable content or fragile SEO. If the foundations remain sound, [focused website optimisation](/en/services/optimisation-site-web/) is often more economical.

A [technical SEO and website audit](/en/services/technical-seo-audit-brussels/) separates what needs rebuilding from what can be improved without starting over.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does a website redesign take?

A well-scoped service-website redesign often takes three to six weeks at Horde. Allow six to twelve weeks when content must be rewritten, several languages or teams are involved, or the project includes significant integrations.

### Can a website be redesigned without losing SEO traffic?

Yes, provided the team inventories the URLs, preserves useful content, prepares redirects and monitors the site after launch. An improvised migration remains one of the biggest redesign risks.

### Why do agency quotes vary so widely?

Because they rarely include exactly the same work. Ask who is responsible for content, UX, SEO, testing, training and post-launch support.

### How much extra does a multilingual website cost?

Multilingual support does not automatically double the budget, but it adds content, metadata, testing and coordination. Professional translation is also a separate cost when the client does not supply it.

### When is optimisation enough?

When the technical and editorial foundations remain healthy. Improving speed, clarifying two service pages and simplifying a form can create more value than replacing the entire website.
