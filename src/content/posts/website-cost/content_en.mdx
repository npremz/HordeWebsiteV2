## Start from what the site has to do, not from the page count

Two five-page websites can differ in price by a factor of four. The page count says very little. What costs money is the work the site does when nobody is watching: convincing a stranger, taking a payment, managing a customer account.

## The AI or builder route: a few hundred euros, then your time

Wix, Framer, Webflow and Lovable can generate a site from a description that you then edit. You pay a subscription. Here is what the entry plan that lets you publish on your own domain costs over two years.

| Tool | Plan | Listed price | Over 24 months |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Framer | Basic, billed yearly | €10 per month | €240 |
| Wix | Light, billed yearly | €14 per month | €336 |
| Webflow | Basic, billed yearly | $15 per month | $360 |
| Lovable | Pro, billed yearly | €21 per month | €504 |

These prices exclude tax: VAT is added at checkout based on your billing address. They change as soon as the site needs to do more:

- **To sell**, Wix requires at least the Essentiel plan (€25 per month, €600 over two years); the Light plan does not take payments. On 18 November, Wix was showing a 50% discount on the first term, which does not apply to renewals.
- **For a blog or a catalogue**, Webflow requires the Premium plan ($25 per month). Framer’s Basic plan stops at 30 pages and 2 content collections; beyond that, Pro costs €30 per month, or €720 over two years.
- **To work as a team**, Framer charges for each additional editor, from €20 per month.
- **With Lovable**, credits pay for building, running the app and hosting its backend. Every iteration uses part of the Pro plan’s 100 monthly credits. Beyond that, you top up.
- **The domain name** is free for the first year with Wix, then paid. Framer includes one on yearly billing.

On paper, then, the AI or builder route is the cheapest. Two costs, however, appear on no pricing page.

### The first hidden cost: your time

Generating a first version takes a few minutes. Getting it right takes far longer: rewriting the copy, choosing images, fixing the mobile version, connecting the form, sorting out SEO.

The maths is simple: hours spent multiplied by what your hour is worth. If you spend 20 hours on it and your hour is worth €60, your site has cost you €1,200 on top of the subscription. That is an example, not an average: run the numbers with your own figures. For many freelancers and small businesses, this is the line that decides.

### The second hidden cost: leaving

A site built on a builder lives on that builder. The day you need to switch tools, the question is not the subscription but what you can take with you.

| Tool | Can you take the site and host it elsewhere? |
| --- | --- |
| Wix | No. The site has to [stay on Wix’s servers](https://support.wix.com/en/article/exporting-or-embedding-your-wix-site-elsewhere); only the domain can be managed elsewhere. |
| Framer | Unclear. One help page says [there is no HTML export](https://www.framer.com/help/articles/can-i-export-my-website-to-html-and-self-host-it/) for self-hosting, another says [you can download your site’s files](https://www.framer.com/help/articles/porting-your-data-from-framer/). |
| Webflow | Partly. [Code export](https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/33961386739347-How-do-I-export-my-Webflow-site-code) requires a paid Workspace plan, and CMS content, forms and the shop do not come with it. |
| Lovable | Yes. [The code is yours](https://docs.lovable.dev/introduction/faq) and syncs with your own GitHub repository. |

A site you cannot take with you gets rebuilt by hand the day you leave. It is a deferred cost, but a real one.

## Freelancer or agency: what you are actually buying

Between the builder and the agency sits the freelancer. We do not publish a “market” range for freelancers: prices vary too much, and we have no reliable data to put numbers on them. Their price is usually built the same way as an agency’s, a rate multiplied by a number of days.

The real difference is not the legal status but what the price includes. Before comparing two quotes, ask three questions:

1. **Who does what?** Design, development, copy, SEO: one person or several, and who checks whose work.
2. **Who owns the site at delivery?** The code, the domain, the hosting and the accounts should all be in your name.
3. **What happens after launch?** Can you edit the content yourself, and what does a change you cannot make cost?

At Horde, our answers are on the pricing page: the code is documented and delivered to your account, we do not resell hosting at a markup, and we train your team to handle day-to-day content. That is part of what the gap with a subscription pays for.

## When the price goes up: finishing what AI started

A site generated in ten minutes costs little at first. The price goes up later, when it needs reworking: the page does not convert, form submissions never arrive, the mobile version is confusing, nobody understands the code any more.

It is not a code problem. In [our comparison of AI coding tools](/en/blog/best-ai-for-coding/), we found that the code from the best models has become good enough. The experience has not: AI adds sections, effects and copy that look convincing without serving what visitors came to do.

That is the work we offer. We keep what works, strip out the noise and rework the journeys that matter. Depending on the state of the site, that means [optimization](/en/services/website-optimization-brussels/) at €199 excl. VAT per day or a redesign from €999 excl. VAT. Starting with AI is not a mistake. Just know that the bill does not end on launch day.

## What will weigh on website prices in 2027

We do not know how prices will move. Here is what we will be watching all year, because each of these changes what a site has to do, and therefore what it costs.

- **Credit-based billing.** Lovable and Framer now meter AI use in credits. The price of a site then follows the number of attempts, not just the plan you picked.
- **Accessibility for online shops.** Since 28 June 2025, the [European Accessibility Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32019L0882) has applied to e-commerce, with an exemption for microenterprises. A shop rebuilt in 2027 has to work with a keyboard and a screen reader, and that work is part of the price.
- **Portability.** The [EU Data Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/2854/oj) has applied since September 2025 and requires cloud service providers to make switching easier. We will see whether builders genuinely open up site export.
- **The quality of what AI generates.** If generators learn to produce less noise and better journeys, the cost of reworking will fall. So far, that is not what we see.

## Before you ask for a quote

1. Write down in one sentence what the site has to do for you.
2. List what it must handle on its own: payments, bookings, accounts, content to update.
3. Estimate how much time you are willing to put in, and what that time is worth.
4. Check what you can take with you if you change tool or provider.
5. Compare options over two years, not over the first month.
